Mattress

Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026

The short version

Mattress keeps your financial data on your phone. There is no Mattress account and no Mattress server. The app sends the developer nothing: no analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking, no advertising, and no copy of your records.

What the app collects, how and why

Mattress only handles what you put into it or connect to it: the accounts, records, categories, notes and labels you type or import from a file; the transactions, balances, trades and positions your bank and broker connections bring in; payment notifications you choose to capture; the questions and photos you give the assistant; and whether you bought Mattress Pro, which the app checks with Apple or Google. It uses them only to show and organise your money on your phone, to run the features you turn on (categorising records, answering your questions, syncing, reminders, backups), and for nothing else. None of it is sold, used for advertising or tracking, or sent to the developer.

What stays on your phone

Accounts, records, categories, rules, recurring payments, debts, investments and settings are stored in a local database on the device. Passphrases, bank keys, broker sessions and keys, AI keys and the ChatGPT sign-in are kept in the phone's secure storage, or encrypted on the phone with a key kept there, and never go into a Mattress backup. Deleting a record hides it but keeps a deletion marker with its contents on the phone, so a bank sync cannot bring it back. Uninstalling the app deletes its database. Like other apps, the app's data on the phone, including captured payment notifications, can be part of your phone's own backup (iCloud or a computer) if you have one turned on; keys and sign-ins in secure storage can only be restored to the same phone. On iPhone, items in secure storage can outlive an uninstall, so disconnect each service in the app first if you want its keys removed.

Optional features that talk to other services

These are off until you turn them on, and each one uses your own account with that service. Two need no account: backup to a folder, and investment prices, which are fetched as soon as a holding has a symbol. Data goes straight from your phone to the service, never through the developer. Each service receives it under its own terms and privacy policy, which give your data the same or equal protection as this policy: they use it to provide what you asked for, keep their service safe and meet the law, and do not sell it. Where a service may use it for more, such as Yahoo Finance or a free Google key below, this policy says so and it is your choice.

  • Google Drive backup. Backups are encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM using your passphrase before they are uploaded, so Google cannot read them. See the Google section below.
  • Backup to a folder. Backups are encrypted on the phone with AES-256-GCM using your passphrase before they are written to a folder you pick. See the folder section below.
  • Bank sync. Uses Enable Banking with credentials you create. The app sends Enable Banking the bank and country you pick, the account and session identifiers it gave, and the dates of the transactions to fetch, and receives your bank accounts, balances and transactions, which it stores on the phone. After the bank login, your browser opens a page on this website that passes the one-time login code back to the app and sends it nowhere else. The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, which receives that request and, like any web host, logs visitors' IP addresses for security. If you turn on "Save raw bank data", the replies of every bank and broker sync are kept on the phone in one file that you can share yourself; it goes only where you send it and into your phone's own backups.
  • Broker connections. Trade Republic, Trading 212 and Robinhood connect directly from the phone. Your Trade Republic phone number and PIN, your Trading 212 API key and secret, and your Robinhood email and password are sent only to that broker, with an identifier the app makes for this phone and its time zone (Trade Republic) or a device token (Robinhood), and any one-time code their login asks for. The PIN and password are never saved. The Trading 212 key and the broker sessions stay on the phone, in secure storage or encrypted with a key kept there. The app receives your cash movements, trades and positions and stores them on the phone.
  • ChatGPT sign-in. You sign in on OpenAI's own page. Signing in sends OpenAI the sign-in request, an identifier for this installation and, when you sign in again, your email; none of your records. The app keeps the sign-in and your account email in the phone's secure storage, and uses your ChatGPT plan for AI requests instead of an API key.
  • AI categorisation. Sends the merchant name, whether the money came in or went out, and your category names and types (and whether each counts in reports), with random IDs to match the answers, to the AI provider you choose: OpenAI through your ChatGPT plan, or Anthropic, OpenAI or Google with your own API key. Amounts, dates, accounts, notes and labels are never sent. Nothing is sent until you allow that provider, see "AI services" below.
  • Assistant. Runs on your ChatGPT plan. When you use it, your questions, any photos you attach, and what it needs from your records to answer, including merchants, amounts, dates, categories, accounts, notes, holdings and debts, are sent to OpenAI. Nothing is sent until you allow it, see "AI services" below. The app asks OpenAI not to store the chat, and the chat is not saved on the phone.
  • Computer access. Lets an AI app on your computer, such as Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex or Cursor, read your records and propose changes, using your own account with that app. It works only while the Computer access screen is open, and only over your Wi-Fi: the phone serves it on its Wi-Fi address, protected by a code shown on that screen, and no server of ours is involved. What the AI asks for from your records, including merchants, amounts, dates, categories, accounts and notes, goes from the phone to the AI app on your computer, and from there to that app's AI provider under its own terms. Nothing is sent to the developer. Changes are made only when you tap Apply on the phone.
  • Investment prices. Sends the ticker symbols of your investments to Yahoo Finance to fetch prices, with the dates of the price history needed, which start at your first trade. Searching for a symbol or syncing a broker portfolio sends the text or ISIN being looked up. Nothing else is sent. Yahoo receives these requests under its own privacy policy and may use the request's IP address, for example for advertising.
  • Payment capture. On Android, reads notifications from the payment app you pick. On iPhone, from iOS 27, a Shortcuts automation you set up passes your bank app's notification to the app. Both happen on the phone, to create records, and the notifications are not sent anywhere. If you allow the assistant, it can read a captured payment waiting for review (app, merchant, amount, currency and date) to answer you, and that goes to OpenAI like the rest of what it reads. If you allow Computer access, the AI app on your computer can read it too.

AI services

Before the app sends any of your records, questions or photos to an AI service for the first time, it asks your permission. It names who receives the data (OpenAI, Anthropic or Google, or for Computer access the AI app on your computer), says what is sent and when, and offers "Allow" or "Not now". If you choose "Not now", nothing is sent and that feature stays off; the rest of the app works as before. AI categorisation asks once for each provider, and the assistant and Computer access ask once each. After that, data is sent when you ask, and, if you turn on Automatic, AI categorisation also sends records without a category by itself while the app is open, including ones already on your phone. You can change any of these choices at any time in More > AI sharing. Turning one off stops future requests; to delete what a company already received, use that company's own controls or contact it. The choice is kept on the phone only, so a new phone asks again.

For AI categorisation and the assistant, the data is sent straight from your phone to that company, over an encrypted connection, only to get the answer you asked for: category suggestions, or a reply from the assistant. Computer access works differently, see below. It is not sent to the developer. Like every service above, each company gives it the same or equal protection as this policy. Whether a company may also use it to improve its models depends on choices you make with it: your ChatGPT data controls, or a free Google key, below.

  • OpenAI, through your ChatGPT plan. Covered by your ChatGPT account's terms and data controls, including whether your chats may be used to improve OpenAI's models. The app asks OpenAI not to store the request. OpenAI privacy policy.
  • Anthropic, OpenAI or Google with your own API key. Covered by that company's API terms. Anthropic and OpenAI do not use API data to train their models unless you agree to it. Google does not for paid Gemini API keys, or for any key in the European Economic Area, Switzerland or the UK; elsewhere, with a free key, Google may use what is sent to improve its products and people may read it, and Google asks not to send personal information that way, so use a paid key. Anthropic privacy policy, OpenAI privacy policy, Gemini API terms.
  • Computer access. The AI app you connect on your computer sends what it reads to its own AI service, under that service's terms. The phone serves it to that computer over plain HTTP on your Wi-Fi, not through the internet, and the computer's app makes its own connection to its AI service. The Computer access screen asks your permission before it starts, and only a computer with the code shown there can connect.

Google user data

Google Drive backup asks for one permission, drive.file. It lets the app see and change only the files it created itself, in a folder called "Mattress backups". A folder made by the older Money Manager app keeps its old name, "Money Manager backups". The app cannot see anything else in your Drive.

The app uses this access only to upload, list, download and remove its own encrypted backups. The data is not sent to the developer, not shared with anyone, not used for advertising and not used to train AI models.

Mattress's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To stop it, disconnect Google Drive in the app, or remove Mattress at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Backups already in your Drive stay there until you delete them.

Backup to a folder

You pick the folder in the phone's own folder picker, and the app gets access to that one folder only. On Android this is a folder grant the system keeps; on iPhone it is a bookmark to the folder. The app can read the folder's file list and write, read and delete its own backup files there. It never deletes files it did not write.

Whatever syncs that folder, such as iCloud Drive, Nextcloud, Syncthing or a cloud drive app, is your own service, under its own terms. Your data never goes through the developer.

The folder backup passphrase is kept in the phone's secure storage and never goes into a Mattress backup. Turning folder backup off forgets the passphrase and the folder. Backups already in the folder stay there until you delete them.

Support emails

If you email support, the developer receives your email address and what you write. It is kept only to answer you, deleted when it is no longer needed, and never shared.

Children

Mattress is not directed at children under 13.
